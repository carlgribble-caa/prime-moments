Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit "Recovering the primes in a dyadic interval from exact moments: two
constructive routes and an obstruction theorem" for consideration in Research in Number Theory.

Two companion manuscripts (cited in the paper, same author) construct exact "moment channels"
for the primes in (m, 2m]: integer power sums obtained from divisor-lattice data with no prime
input, and weighted logarithmic moments obtained from the primes below the window. This paper
closes the circle: the moments determine the window primes individually. Theorem 1 recovers them
from r + 1 integer moments via Newton's identities and integer root reading, with no primality
test anywhere in the pipeline; Theorem 2 recovers them from a single real moment — Chebyshev's
functional theta(2m) − theta(m) — delivered to about log_2(product of window primes) bits, by
rounding and window trial division; a Prony decoder interpolates. Against these, Theorem 3 proves
a pigeonhole floor: any scheme whose decoder sees at most D bits, correct on every r-subset of
the window, needs D at least log_2 C(m, r), which diverges — while the companions' determination
of a single aggregate from O(log m) moments sits near the same floor at entropy O(log m). The
model restriction that makes the folklore "no formula for the primes" well-posed (excluding
Mills- and Willans-type decoders) is stated and discussed explicitly. All constructions are
machine-verified — at m = 500 both routes recover the 73 primes of (500, 1000] exactly — with
code at https://github.com/carlgribble-caa/prime-moments.

On the companion manuscripts: both are publicly available (doi:10.5281/zenodo.21769102 and
doi:10.5281/zenodo.21769104) and are attached as supplementary files for the referees'
convenience; each is under review elsewhere as an independent paper. Only two displayed facts are
imported from them — stated in full in Section 2 of the present paper, which is self-contained
given those displays — so the referee's reliance on the companions is limited to the provenance
of two identities.
