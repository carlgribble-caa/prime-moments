Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit "Certified exact sums of primes over dyadic intervals via a ladder of
generalized factorials" for consideration in Experimental Mathematics.

The paper constructs generalized factorials with Chebyshev-polynomial exponents whose prime
valuations isolate the primes in a dyadic window (m, 2m]. Each polynomial weight yields one exact
linear functional of those primes, computable from the primes below m alone; finitely many such
moments, closed by a certified interval-arithmetic sandwich and the integrality of the target,
determine the exact sum of the window primes. The method is elementary end to end — its ancestor
is Chebyshev's 1852 central-binomial argument, which the paper recasts as rung zero of the ladder
— and it is verified by exact certified computations at m = 10^3, 10^4, and 10^5, reported with
sandwich widths and cross-checked against independent ground truth. All code is openly available
at https://github.com/carlgribble-caa/prime-moments. A companion manuscript by the same author is
cited for context and cross-checks only; the present paper is mathematically self-contained.

I believe the paper fits the journal's remit precisely: a new computational method, developed and
validated experimentally, with every claim machine-verified and reproducible. The contribution is
methodological rather than asymptotic — no speed records are claimed, and the paper says so
plainly; its value is a certified, minimal-trust elementary route to exact answers.

text under my direction; a full statement in the publisher's required format (tool, version,
